# Act of 1911
- Insurance contributions paid by worker and employer were unrelated to past employment
    - no monetary incentive to substitute wage reductions for layoffs,
    because benefits were external to worker and employer
- while benefits were tied to age range and sex, they were not affected by wage
    - therefore they had a larger effect to lower wage people
- very lean rules on what makes up the required 6 days of continous unemployment
allowing many people to receive benefits on the first day of unemployment, if there
were 2 3-day periods of unemployment withing he last 10 weeks

# Time series evidence
- people sometimes become "unemployed" to search for the best valued use of their 
labour in so called "search unemployment"
- when unemployment rose because of a shock, the disappearance of it doesn't
immediately eliminate excess unemployment
- "business is bad" periods cause trusted employees to be retained, which are then
used for tasks of dismissed employees and maintanance, causing output to be lower
relative to the trend
