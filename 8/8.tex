    \begin{exercise}[Exploring t-SNE and UMAP Parameter Space]
    In t-SNE, perplexity is a measure on the number of neighbours each point considers when building the probability distribution.
    Consequently, setting a low perplexity focuses on local structure and builds smaller clusters.
    A high perplexity generally results in bigger clusters and overall less deformation in comparison to the original dataset.
    Therefore, size, shape, and distances between individual (sub-) clusters may not be important for the information extracted from a plot.
    Additionally, the randomness induced by subsequent t-SNE runs may result in totally different plots with different takeaways.

    In UMAP, \(n_\text{neighbours}\) is the number of nearest neighbours used to construct the local connectivity graph.
    This again focuses on a trade-off between the importance of local and global neighbours.
    In this way this parameter is similar to the t-SNE perplexity.
    \(min_\text{dist}\) controls the minimal distance between points in the resulting distribution.
    Therefore, both parameters control cluster size and distances.
    Subsequently, the consequences on t-SNE can also be made with UMAP.
    \end{exercise}
